Thesis // The Historical Parallel

What Dee Hock Built for Consumer Retail in 1958

Consumer commerce had the exact same gridlock 65 years ago.

1950s: Bilateral Store Credit
Fragmented, High-Risk Store Books

Every store (Sears, local grocers, gas stations) maintained individual customer ledgers. Merchants took 100% of the customer default risk, suffered from uncollectible debts, and customers carried dozens of disparate credit books.

Outcome: Inefficient, localized, high default rate, zero scale.
1970+: VISA Multilateral Clearing
The Universal Settlement Protocol

BankAmericard (VISA) unified all merchants and consumers into a single clearinghouse. Credit was decoupled from individual merchants; transactions were netted multilaterally; and settlements cleared with near-zero friction.

Outcome: A $14 Trillion/year global transaction network with 50%+ operating margins.
